## Method

Each case names a medicine and the condition it was later used for. The engine builds its candidate list for that condition and the report gives the medicine's rank. Signals: side effects in drug labels (OnSIDES) that the curated mapping points to the condition, and FAERS reports in which the condition is reported less often than expected. Ranking (key K1): has a label signal, the Wilson lower bound of the share of the medicine's labels that list the effect, the number of label regions, the number of such labels, has an inverse FAERS signal, the FAERS report count; never a ratio. Equal positions share a rank range.

- **Primary run** (later use excluded where possible): US labels only. Two variants for the case medicine: (a) remove its labels whose openFDA indications text names the later use; (b) also remove its labels that openFDA does not have. The published primary result is the worse of the two, and a hit counts only if both are hits.
- **Sensitivity run**: every label of every region, nothing removed (the list a researcher sees).
- **Hit**: worst rank within the top 10. A hit among 10 or fewer candidates is uninformative. **Chance**: the probability that a random candidate of the condition lands in the top 10 (10 / candidates); **percentile**: worst rank / candidates. No p-value is given.
- FAERS: The FAERS reports of this window were sent after the later use was known; this number does not exclude it.

## Limits

- The side-effect mapping was written knowing these cases, so this check is not blind. The ranking key and three mapping rows were changed after the first run's case ranks were seen.
- Only known successes were tested: there are no negative controls, so the check cannot estimate false positives or precision.
- n = 13 cases; 4 cannot be reached by construction.
- The labels that stay in the primary run are today's texts of the original-use products; their side-effect sections may have been written after the later use was known. The primary run removes labels that name the later use; it does not rebuild the labels as they were.
- Labels are not independent: generic copies repeat the same text, so label counts are an ordering device, not a sample.
- Inverse signals need many reports (at least 3 reports and an upper bound below 1), so they exist only for heavily reported medicines; pairs with no report at all are not evaluated.
- Side effects come from OnSIDES, which extracts them from drug labels with a language model; some are missed and some are wrong.
- Reporting bias: an event that is already known for a medicine is reported more often (notoriety bias), and a condition that is the reason a medicine is given is often recorded as the indication rather than as an event (indication bias). Both can create or hide a lower-than-expected count.
- Janus does not check whether a candidate is already an approved use of the medicine.
